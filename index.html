Staying at the Top of AFL

From Season Results to Lasting Success

I compared all 18 AFL clubs across 14 seasons (2012–2025) to explore which teams stayed near the top and how that consistency translated into premierships. Following their ladder positions, finals appearances and titles reveals different paths to success, from years of regular contention to a few outstanding seasons.

Finals appearances vary across clubs.

A season can be a breakthrough, a rebuild or a missed opportunity. The ladder puts every club’s journey on the same scale, revealing who kept returning to the top and whose strong seasons were separated by longer periods of struggle. Start with the full fourteen-year picture, then look at how often those clubs reached finals.

The ladder ranks clubs after the regular season; first is best. The finals series follows the regular season and decides the premiership winner, so a high ladder finish and a title are different achievements.

Success varies across states and cities.

The club stories also have a geography. Victoria has ten clubs, while each other state represented in the competition has two, giving Victoria more opportunities to supply finalists. Comparing each state’s share of clubs reaching finals shows how widely success was spread relative to its club base.

More finals appearances do not guarantee titles.

Getting to finals keeps the door open, but winning the premiership is a different test. Hawthorn and Richmond each won three titles during this period, while Geelong reached finals more often than either and won once. The clubs that stayed in contention longest were not always the ones that collected the most trophies.

Club win rates rise and fall over time.

A fourteen-season average can hide the order in which success arrived. Brisbane’s later rise and West Coast’s decline are easier to see when the seasons stay in sequence. Compare those paths first, then the spread of each club’s results and the change between two equal seven-season periods.

A steady record can mean sustained success or sustained struggle. To understand consistency, consider both how much results varied and whether the club usually finished near the top.

Different clubs lead on different measures of success.

Geelong’s repeated finals appearances show sustained contention; Hawthorn and Richmond’s titles show how much a club can achieve in a concentrated period. Brisbane’s rise to consecutive premierships in 2024 and 2025 adds another path: success built after years near the bottom. Who stayed at the top depends on whether we value regular contention, winning the biggest games, or improvement over time.

Win-rate changes compare pooled wins divided by games in 2012–2018 and 2019–2025; pp means percentage points. Games won uses actual games as its denominator; the other club-comparison measures use fourteen seasons.
